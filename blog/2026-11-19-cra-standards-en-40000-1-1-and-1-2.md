On 19 November 2026, CEN-CENELEC announced that the first European standards supporting the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) are available: **EN 40000-1-1** (Part 1-1, vocabulary) and **EN 40000-1-2** (Part 1-2, principles, product risk management and lifecycle activities), both in the series on cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements. The announcement says that "These are among the first standards developed under Standardization Request M/606", through which the European Commission asked CEN, CENELEC and ETSI to develop standards supporting the CRA.

Both standards were developed in CEN-CLC/JTC 13/WG 9, the working group for horizontal cybersecurity standards for products with digital elements. CEN-CENELEC says EN 40000-1-1 sets common terms and definitions for the EN 40000 series, and that "EN 40000-1-2 establishes a common foundation for cybersecurity risk management", bringing together "general cybersecurity principles, baseline requirements for managing cybersecurity risks and activities covering the entire product life cycle."

{/* truncate */}

The announcement says the standards "can be found and obtained through national standards catalogues", "subject to the applicable national publication schedules." It does not give national dates. It also does not mention citation in the Official Journal of the EU, harmonized-standard status, or presumption of conformity. This post makes no claim about any of those.

For supply-chain assurance, the significance is practical rather than legal. Manufacturers asking suppliers for risk-management and lifecycle evidence have so far had to describe what they want in their own words. A published horizontal vocabulary and a common lifecycle risk-management standard give both sides a shared reference for those requests.

The useful question is not "is the supplier compliant with EN 40000?" It is "which lifecycle and risk-management records do we need from this supplier, and can we now describe them in terms the supplier also uses?"

## What the announcement says, and what it does not

| Source statement | Handbook interpretation |
|---|---|
| EN 40000-1-1 provides vocabulary for the EN 40000 series | Supplier contracts and questionnaires may be able to use the same terms as the standard, which reduces ambiguity in evidence requests. |
| EN 40000-1-2 covers principles, product risk management and lifecycle activities "covering the entire product life cycle" | Risk-management evidence may be expected to cover the whole lifecycle, not only design and release. |
| The standards are among the first under Standardization Request M/606 | They are part of the European standardization work that supports the CRA. |
| Availability is "subject to the applicable national publication schedules" | Suppliers in different countries may get access at different times, so early requests should allow for that. |
| Further horizontal standards on vulnerability handling and generic cybersecurity requirements are being developed, and FprEN 40000-1-3 and the first vertical standards are expected soon | Evidence requests built on these two parts will need revisiting as more parts are published. |

The announcement does not say that these standards are harmonized standards, that they are cited in the Official Journal, or that they give presumption of conformity. The interpretation column is handbook guidance based on the announcement alone; this post does not summarize the content of the standards themselves. The [EU Cyber Resilience Act](/standards-threats/standards-regulations/eu-cyber-resilience-act/) page explains why harmonized standards and presumption of conformity matter, and why they do not remove the need for underlying evidence.

## Lifecycle and risk-management evidence to request

A lifecycle risk-management standard is most useful to a buyer when it changes what the buyer asks a supplier to produce. Requests that may now be easier to frame include:

| Evidence request | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The supplier's product risk assessment for the supplied component, with its scope and date | Shows which risks the supplier considered and for which product boundary. |
| Lifecycle activities the supplier performs, from design through end of support | Shows whether risk management continues after release. |
| How risk assessments are updated after vulnerabilities, changes or incidents | Shows that the assessment is maintained, not produced once. |
| The terms and definitions the supplier uses in its documentation | Reduces disputes about what key terms in the supplier's evidence mean. |
| Records the supplier will retain and share, and for how long | Supports later audit, incident response and customer assurance. |

These are handbook suggestions, not requirements from the standards. The [Supply Chain Security Lifecycle Map](/practices-controls/supply-chain-security-lifecycle-map/) shows how evidence needs change across lifecycle stages.

## Weak and stronger supplier answers

Once suppliers know the standards exist, some will cite them without showing anything. Buyers should expect the reference to come with records.

Weak answer:

> We follow the EN 40000 series.

Stronger answer:

> Our product risk assessment for this component is dated and scoped to the version you receive, uses the terms defined in EN 40000-1-1, and covers the lifecycle activities described in EN 40000-1-2 from design to end of support. We update it after significant changes and vulnerabilities, and we can share the current version and change history under the agreed terms.

The stronger answer still makes no claim of conformity. It gives the buyer a scoped, dated artifact to review. [Supplier and Procurement Assurance](/practices-controls/supplier-assurance-procurement/) covers how to write requests like this into contracts and renewal checks.
